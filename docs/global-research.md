# Global selection and historical contexts

Prepared 2026-10-01. This dataset adds eighteen thinkers or attributed traditions and sixteen historical contexts to a predominantly European and North American introductory timeline. It is a curated starting point, not a world canon, population sample, or ranking of importance. Islamic, Chinese, Indian, Japanese, Jewish, Caribbean, and African intellectual histories are presented as philosophical projects with their own questions. Maimonides and Fanon deliberately cross geographic and linguistic classifications.

## Dates, works, and locations

All numeric years use astronomical numbering: 0 means 1 BCE, -1 means 2 BCE. Thus -550 is 551 BCE. Approximation flags apply to conventional intervals as well as uncertain years. A plotted work can be a composition, publication, or later collection; the note explains that distinction. Unknown life dates are represented explicitly: Mozi has no established birth or death date and is displayed only at his c.430 BCE floruit anchor (-429). Xunzi’s death is unknown after 238 BCE; his activity endpoint (-237) marks the latest attested activity bound and does not stand in for a death year. These historical unknowns never mean that a thinker is currently living.

- Laozi’s identity and lifespan are not established. The c.600–500 BCE display is a traditional attribution; the Daodejing is a composite text commonly associated with fourth–third-century BCE formation. No alleged court-library location is plotted as documented residence.
- The Buddha’s c.480–400 BCE interval follows one reconstruction; c.563–483 BCE is a familiar alternative. Discourses were preserved orally and collected later. Lumbini, Bodh Gaya, and Sarnath are traditional associations, not a securely dated modern itinerary.
- Confucius’s Analects and the received Zhuangzi are layered collections. Their dates must not be read as authorial publication dates.
- Nāgārjuna’s dates and regional association are uncertain. Śaṅkara’s early-eighth-century chronology competes with other dates, including the familiar 788–820; later monastery narratives are not asserted as secure biography.
- Some locations are explicitly traditional or regional rather than documented city residence. Modern coordinates locate approximate associations; current countries and boundaries are orientation aids and imply neither ancient nationality nor endorsement of territorial claims.
- Reception intervals are broad editorial summaries of schools, translations, institutions, or movements. The ending year 2026 marks continuing reception at preparation, not continuous influence of fixed strength.

## Comparative coordinates

The three axes are editorial lenses in [-1, 1]: matter → mind, experience → reason, individual → collective. They are not measurements, survey results, or scholarly consensus. Each philosopher has a separate rationale for every axis. Near-center coordinates frequently mean that an opposition fits the tradition poorly, rather than that the thinker takes a settled middle position. Dao, emptiness, li/qi, and nondual brahman cannot be translated directly into modern European substance categories. “Mind” also does not mean merely private preference. Classification is deliberately debatable and should remain visible beside the point.

## Historical events

Events distinguish chronology, cultural context, and identifiable intellectual responses. A related philosopher can be an earlier author received later, a participant, or a later analyst; the description identifies which relationship is intended. It is not a causal graph. The French Revolution’s Rousseau link describes later reception. Aśoka’s Buddha link describes institutional reception after the Buddha’s lifetime. World War I links Russell’s antiwar activism and Wittgenstein’s service without asserting that the war produced a single doctrine. Holocaust and World War II are shown separately because political responsibility and genocide require specific context.

Martin Luther’s 1517 theses and Martin Luther King Jr.’s 1963 Birmingham letter are separate entries. The former concerns Reformation controversies; the latter concerns civil rights, just law, and civil disobedience. Printing is specifically labeled a European expansion and acknowledges earlier East Asian technologies. Colonialism’s displayed 1757–1962 interval is a selected sequence, not the total history of colonial domination. Decolonization and suffrage dates also describe selected phases rather than universal completion.

## Limits and next coverage

The eighteen additions cannot represent the diversity of African, Indigenous American, Oceanian, Southeast Asian, Tibetan, Persian, and South Asian traditions. Buddhist schools beyond the selected figures, Jain thought, Islamic thinkers beyond the selected figures, women in these traditions, oral philosophical practices, and contemporary non-Western scholarship remain serious gaps. A useful next pass would add traditions on their own source and attribution terms rather than force every oral or collective lineage into a single-person biography. The qualitative region list also compresses Central Asia, the Caribbean, Tibet, and Southeast Asia; this is a display limitation rather than a judgment of importance.
